A game tester that plays, notices, and writes it up

Three agents test a Godot platformer. The Explorer plays it with random key presses while a local CLIP model checks it is still looking at the game. The Inspector flags stretches where the screen jumps or stops responding. The Reporter asks a vision LLM for a bug report. Below are three real runs from this morning, two of them as replays, and every number on this page comes from the runs' own log files.

How it works

Explorer

explorer.py

Plays with a seeded mix of random keys and short committed runs left or right. It aims for 10 fps but records about 3.3, because pyautogui waits 0.1 s after every key event, so 30 frames is about 9 s.

Guard

explorer.py, perception.py

Before each key press it checks that the game window has focus, and every fifth tick it asks CLIP (ViT-B/32, on the CPU) whether the frame is the game. If either check fails, it presses nothing and tries three times to get the game back, then stops.

Inspector

inspector.py, calibrate.py

Runs after the run. A sudden jump is a frame change over 43.0. Unresponsive means a 30-frame median change under 1.52 while at least half the keys are moves. Both thresholds come from one clean minute of play. CLIP decides whether an anomaly is a game bug or a harness problem.

Reporter

reporter.py

Sends four frames and the Inspector's summary to a vision LLM and asks for a JSON bug report: title, severity, reproduction steps, suspected cause, and whether it is a real bug at all.

What it can't do yet

  1. Explore with purpose. The key sequence is random with a fixed seed. There is no learned policy and no coverage goal.
  2. See a gameplay bug that doesn't change the whole screen. A second planted bug, a ledge you fall through, is invisible to pixel change and to a whole-frame CLIP check.
  3. Trust its thresholds. They come from one clean minute, and the jump threshold has already fired on a camera move.
  4. Keep other windows out of the evidence. A half-covered frame passed the 0.5 cut-off in run C. Capturing the game window itself, not a region of the screen, would fix that.
  5. Confirm what the Reporter says. Its causes and reproduction steps are unverified, and nothing replays the inputs.
  6. Run anywhere but Windows, on anything but this one game, from outside the engine.
